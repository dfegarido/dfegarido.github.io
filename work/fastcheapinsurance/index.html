Editorial pages on a static build

What it is

A plain-language guide site that explains car, home, health, life and pet insurance, and how to pay less for it. Sixty-nine URLs in the sitemap: fifty-one articles, seven topic hubs, a rates page, a renewal checklist, a blog index, and the trust pages that make the rest checkable.

Three decisions that shaped it

The content lives in one place, and the site is generated from it.

1. Static pages, generated from templates

Within a day of starting, the site was rebuilt as static HTML, CSS and JavaScript, assembled by a small Node build script with shared partials and templates. Article pages do not need to be assembled per request, so the whole site is cacheable at the edge.

2. Articles are written once, in Leadmailer

The guides are managed in the sending platform's content API and pulled into the build. One editorial workspace produces content for more than one property, instead of each site growing its own CMS and its own copy of the same article.

3. It publishes an llms.txt next to the sitemap

Alongside sitemap.xml and robots.txt, the site states in llms.txt what it is and how it may be read by machines. The robots.txt goes further and carries explicit content signals, because being read by a crawler is now a decision rather than a default.

Outcome, stated honestly

Traffic and conversion sit in the client's analytics, so they are not published here rather than not measured. What is checkable on the site itself: sixty-nine pages, one editorial source, and a build that regenerates them.
