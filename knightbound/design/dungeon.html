Design review · dungeon

The Dungeon map

Menu → Dungeon opens this map. The levels climb a winding path up the dungeon wall, level 1 at the bottom. Tap a level to see what waits there, then Enter; tap the bare wall to put the sheet away and look along the path. Try it in the phone: the glowing level, a cleared one, a locked one.

The path

Three kinds of level

  • Cleared: a gold rim and a tick. Replay any time for its kills and boss drop; its first-clear Souls are already paid.
  • Next: torch-lit and breathing. The map opens scrolled to it, with its sheet up.
  • Locked: dark, the boss in silhouette, a padlock. Clear the level below to open it. Tapping it still shows the sheet, with Enter disabled.

Every node shows its own boss — twenty different ones. The wall changes colour with each of the four depths, and every fifth level is the depth's keeper: a bigger node and a harder boss.

The sheet

What a level tells you

Its boss and that boss's types, the stage it counts as (level 1 = stage 100, +10 a level), the verdict — Ready / Risky / Too strong, from how fast you kill the boss against how fast it kills you (there is no timer in a dungeon) — its depth's four monsters that make up the 4 waves, and the rewards: coins and XP for every kill, an Epic+ gear drop from the boss, and the one-time Souls for a first clear.

How a dungeon boss hits

Toe to toe — unless it's Heavy

On the meadow every hit shoves the knight back. In a dungeon most bosses don't: they hit 75% faster than a stage boss and he takes it standing, swinging back the whole time, so the fight never pauses. Only Heavy bosses knock him back — slow, with a long wind-up you can see coming, then one powerful blow that throws him off his feet. And some are human-shaped, with a scythe: they never stop cutting, 16 cuts a second.

Most bosses75% faster than a stage boss: a blow every 0.9 s instead of 1.6 s (0.65 s if Fast). A red flash and the number, no shove: he keeps fighting.
Heavy bossesOne blow every 1.4 s after a visible wind-up, 2.5x the damage, and it throws him back and down. Forge Golem, Rot Toad, Iron Minotaur, Frost Giant.
Flurry bossesHuman-shaped, with a scythe that never stops: 16 cuts a second, nonstop. Each cut is very light; no shove, so he keeps fighting through it. One number climbs with every cut and starts afresh each second. Grave Reaper, Cinder Witch.

Redesign · draft for review

Bosses with bodies

Today every dungeon boss except the Grave Reaper is its map icon blown up 2x: a front-facing head with chunky 2 px pixels, floating in front of the knight. These drafts are drawn the way the Reaper is: a whole body seen from the side, facing the knight, at the game's own 1x pixel size, with feet on the floor. One per depth, to set the style; the other sixteen follow once these look right. The map keeps the head icons (they read well small).

Bone Warden was: the icon at 2x
Dungeon 1 · Catacombs A skeleton knight a head taller than the knight: horned helm, torn red cape, a greatsword planted point-down. Cyan eye-fire is the only bright thing on him until the blade comes up.
Spore Lord was: the icon at 2x
Dungeon 7 · Fungal Caverns A mushroom giant: the cap is the silhouette, almost as wide as the frame, its spots glowing. Long root arms; the slam bursts a ring of spores.
Iron Minotaur was: the icon at 2x
Dungeon 14 · Molten Forge Hunched forward, head low, horns aimed at you. The double axe rests on his shoulder with its edges still red-hot; the chop throws sparks off the floor.
The Abyss Dragon was: the icon at 2x
Dungeon 20 · keeper of the Frozen Abyss The final keeper is the only four-legged boss: wings raised, tail curled, violet eyes. He rears back, his throat lights, then breathes violet fire rimmed with ice.
The rules they follow
  • The silhouette reads first: fill each one in solid black and it's still a skeleton knight, a mushroom, a bull, a dragon. No two bosses share a body shape.
  • One oversized signature prop: a greatsword, a cap, a double axe, a pair of wings. It's what moves in the attack, so the wind-up is easy to see.
  • Dark body, one hot accent: eyes and the dangerous edge are the brightest pixels (cyan, violet, forge-orange, violet and ice).
  • 1x pixels, big frame: 72x64 like the Reaper, so they tower over the 48x54 knight; detail that doesn't survive at 1x is cut.
  • The hit lands on frame 2, as now: a held wind-up, the swing, the impact with a trail, the recovery.

Built from the advice in How to make pixel art that reads clearly and 10 pixel-art composition rules, and the boss collections on OpenGameArt (looked at for proportion and scale only; nothing traced).

Featured boss · Dungeon 4

The Grave Reaper

Flurry · DodgyA hooded reaper that floats over the crypt floor, scythe raised, eyes burning in the dark of the hood.
His crit: the Death CutMid-flurry he stops dead for a heartbeat — eyes flare into burning crosses, sparks ring the blade (your warning) — then one huge cut sweeps the stage: a white flash, a giant crescent, the crit starburst with a big white number. Worth 2 full blows. The knight staggers where he stands (no shove), and the flurry picks straight back up. About once every 3 seconds (2% a cut).
Why it reads at a glance
  • Silhouette first: the outsized scythe and the wide, torn cape make the shape a reaper before any detail shows.
  • The face stays hidden: the hood is near-black, and only two magenta eyes and a sliver of bone mask catch the light.
  • One hot accent: magenta on the eyes and the blade's edge, against an almost-black body, so the dangerous part is the brightest.
  • He floats: no feet, a ragged hem, a shadow on the floor below, a soul lantern and drifting wisps.
  • The flurry never stops: 16 cuts a second, nonstop; two ghost blades trail every cut, and the arc it swept flashes white and magenta.

Boss types

What makes each boss different

Every boss has its own look and one to three types that change the fight. A depth's fifth boss — its keeper — always has the most.

  • FastEven quicker: a blow every 0.65 s instead of 0.9 s. Still no shove.
  • HeavyThe only bosses that knock him back: slow (one blow every 1.4 s, a long wind-up) but each hits 2.5x and throws him off his feet.
  • ArmoredTakes 30% less damage from everything.
  • Dodgy40% evasion: plain swings often miss (Shadow Step and Omen never do).
  • VenomousIts hits poison the knight: 2% of max HP a second for 4 s.
  • BurningIts hits set the knight burning: 3% of max HP a second for 3 s.
  • FreezingIts hits chill the knight: he attacks 30% slower for 3 s.
  • SummonerAt half HP it calls a pack of 2 of its depth's monsters.
  • EnrageBelow 30% HP it attacks twice as fast.
  • FlurryHuman-shaped, with a scythe that never stops: 16 cuts a second, nonstop, each a very light cut. No shove. One number climbs with every cut and starts afresh each second, instead of a blizzard of numbers.

Who lives down there

Four depths, twenty bosses

Each depth has its own four monsters (the waves are made of them) and five bosses, one per level. Nothing repeats across depths, and nothing from the meadow comes down here.
